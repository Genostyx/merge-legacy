# Completed Work

- **Added the rotating Special Items store and physical Splitter.** The full shop now includes a separate three-slot, four-hour rotating weighted row containing level-eligible dispenser pieces and the rarer unrestricted Splitter. Splitters cost 60 Gems, enter the board or forced-spawn vault, and are dragged onto an unlocked tier-02+ item. A confirmation panel shows the target artwork before consuming the Splitter and replacing the target with two items one tier lower.

**Generated orders are gated by owned dispenser families (2026-08-31).** Player level no longer introduces Stone at level 10 or Glass at level 20 before the player can produce those items. A family becomes eligible for newly issued orders only after the player owns at least Source 01 for it. The eligible-family set is saved per active order alongside its issue level, preventing an existing order from changing when another dispenser is acquired. Legacy saves rebuild missing family snapshots from dispensers already on the board or waiting in the forced-spawn vault.

**Five-day escalating Daily Supply ladder (2026-08-30).** Consecutive claims now follow Day 1 level-scaled credits, Day 2 larger level-scaled credits, Day 3 Bronze Crate, Day 4 Silver Crate, and Day 5+ Gold Crate. Credit days pay 40% and 80% of a typical same-level order respectively, rounded to the nearest five with 10/20 minimums. Day 5 remains visibly labelled `5+` and continues awarding Gold while the streak remains unbroken; missing a day resets the next claim to Day 1. The profile previews the exact next reward with matching credit or crate artwork.

**Infinite forced-spawn vault (2026-08-30).** Mandatory physical rewards use a persistent, unlimited last-in/first-out stack whenever the board cannot accept them. The passive vault appears beside the information panel only while occupied, previews the next item and total count, accepts no taps or manually stored pieces, and automatically flies its top item onto the board as soon as a cell opens. New rewards visibly travel into the vault from their real origin—the order card, profile reward, Daily Supply card, or ring meter—before becoming eligible to leave it. Legacy queued source rewards migrate into the same stack.

**Family collection menu with claimable discovery gems (2026-08-30).** A Collection entry inside the player profile opens all three nine-item family ladders, and unclaimed discovery gems contribute to the profile button's notification badge. Locked slots show `?`, discovered/unclaimed slots use the gem itself as the scrim, and claimed slots reveal their item art. Discovery and claiming persist separately, each claim grants exactly one gem with a particle-and-HUD flight animation, family headers show claimed/total, and legacy saves reconstruct lower-tier history from player-owned board and inventory items without counting locked cells.

**Player progression profile hides XP arithmetic and previews the next real reward (2026-08-30).** The profile retains its numberless progress bar and leveling guidance, exposes no total/current/required XP figures, and shows the next actual milestone crate. The lookup skips levels without milestone rewards instead of falling back to a generic `CRATE` label. Milestone crates no longer wait for a manual profile claim: reaching the level marks the reward claimed and automatically sends it from the player-profile button to the board, or into the forced-spawn vault when the board is full. Older saves deliver any previously unclaimed milestones on load.

**Ring-crate meter cooldown (2026-08-30).** Receiving Bronze, Silver, or Gold starts a 10-minute cooldown stored as an absolute timestamp, so it continues offline. During cooldown the meter accepts no source-run progress, its circular timer drains smoothly, and the card becomes the final item in the horizontally scrollable order queue. It rests beyond the visible right edge and can be reached by swiping to the end; when the timer expires it slides back to its normal far-left lane.

**Ready orders SLIDE to the front again (2026-08-30).** Reported as "implemented before but got removed somehow". It was never removed - the tween is still there and always was. The binding underneath it had inverted.

Cards were bound to SCREEN POSITIONS: `orderCards.map((view, position) => { const queueSlot = orderDisplayOrder[position]; ... })`. So when an order became ready, the leftmost card's CONTENT was rewritten to be that order and no card ever moved. The slide could not fire either - a card's target x was always the x it already had, which takes the `view.root.x === targetX` snap branch every time.

Cards are now bound to QUEUE SLOTS: a card object owns one order for that order's whole life and only its position in the row changes. Pass 1 builds content indexed by slot; pass 2 walks `orderDisplayOrder` accumulating the cursor, so a reorder becomes a change of x and therefore something that can be animated. Ready cards are also brought to the top of the container, so they pass OVER the cards giving way rather than under - sliding behind its neighbours was most of what made the movement hard to follow.

`submitOrderSlot` lost its `orderDisplayOrder` indirection with the change: card index and queue slot can no longer diverge, which was the whole hazard that indirection existed to guard against.

Verified live rather than by inspection, since this is view code with no headless harness. Driving a reorder in the running scene: display order `[0,1,2]` -> `[2,0,1]` creates 3 x-tweens; card 2 travels 435 -> 231 (at 70ms) -> 152 while cards 0 and 1 slide right behind it, keeping their relative order.

CAUTION for future work here: this is the one part of the order bar with no unit test guarding it - `orderDisplaySequence` is tested, but the card-to-slot BINDING is scene code. If reordering ever looks like a pop again, check that binding first.


**Order cards show the item, not its name (2026-08-30).** Requirements were one full item name per line (`WALNUT BLOCK 0/1`); they are now the item's own art with its count beside it, packed side by side on one row. A player scanning an order against their board is comparing shapes, so drawing the shape removes a translation step - the full name still exists in the order info box, which is what that box is for.

Requirement icons use `drawTierIcon` with the same `iconPresentation` normalisation the board applies, so an item is the same size and sits on the same ground line on a card as it does in its cell. `REQ_ICON` is 32: the band between title and rewards is ~35px and the presentation ladder scales art to roughly half its box, so anything smaller is a shape too small to recognise, which would defeat the point. A `dispenser-collects` order has no item to draw and keeps its words.

Two layout traps worth remembering. `drawTierIcon` centres on its origin while the row packer positions by LEFT edge, so an un-offset icon hangs half off the card - the icon is shifted by half a box inside its token. And rows now carry their OWN gap: a row of pictures needs more air than a row of text, and measuring with one gap while packing with another leaves the row misaligned.

Added `src/vite-env.d.ts` and a DEV-only `window.game` handle in `main.ts`, after a stale frame sent me chasing a card-overlap bug that did not exist - the layout was correct and the screenshot was not. Reading the real widths out of the running scene settled it immediately. Verified stripped from the production build.


**Currency symbols replace letter codes (2026-08-30).** New `src/game/ui/CurrencyGlyph.ts` owns all three marks. The glyphs already existed but were welded into their own HUD chips at their own fixed radii, which is why nothing else in the game could show one without redrawing it by hand; they are now drawn centred on the origin at any requested height, so a mark can sit on a text baseline anywhere. `currencyLabel()` pairs a number with its mark as one positionable object, since Phaser text cannot embed a drawn shape mid-string.

Converted: floating receipts, order card reward tokens, the tray's reward chips, the shared SELL/REFILL button, shop offer prices, coin and gem pack rows, the reroll rows, the inventory slot price, and the energy panel's refill and GET buttons. The three HUD chips now call the shared helper instead of their own copies.

The rule applied: a value with a unit gets the mark; prose keeps its words. So description text - the action tray's title and progress lines, every `NOT ENOUGH GEMS` message, panel titles and section headers like `BUY WITH CREDITS` - is unchanged, per the info-box exemption. `orderRewardSummary` in Orders.ts also keeps words; it is referenced only by tests now.

`E` was handled through the call sites rather than by searching strings, since as a bare letter it would have matched inside words. Six tests in `CurrencyGlyph.test.ts` pin that every legacy unit code still maps, that each mark fits its stated box centred on the origin, that it scales linearly, and that it fills the height it claims - the properties that let one helper serve an 8px order card and a 22px HUD chip.


**Generated orders now curve (2026-08-30).** Three separate defects, all measured before they were changed rather than assumed:

- *The curve stopped dead at level 12.* `maxOrderTier` reaches 9 there and cannot rise - there is no tier 10 - so level 12 and level 45 produced identical orders: same tier window (6-9), same line cap (3), same counts. Average work actually FELL across that span (475 -> 302) while rewards kept inflating from `1 + wave * 0.04`. Past the tier cap the curve now comes from COUNT instead, via `maxRequirementCount`: one more copy every eight levels, so the last step is still ahead of a level-36 player rather than saturating at 20. Average work now climbs 463 -> 661 from level 12 to 45, and a test pins it monotonic.
- *Early orders were pinned to a floor.* `Math.max(60, ...)` was the only thing setting the price of anything below level 6 - a one-drop delivery and a sixteen-drop one both paid exactly 60. Floor is now 6, which catches only a lone tier-1 drop.
- *Collections and deliveries swapped relative value by 10x.* A collection paid 2.4x a delivery at level 1 and 0.18x at level 12, so at every point in the game one of the two order types was a trap holding a queue slot. Collection rewards are now priced off `typicalOrderWork(level)` - the same work model the deliveries use - at `COLLECTION_REWARD_SHARE` of it. The ratio now sits at ~1.39x from level 1 to 45, and a test bounds it to 0.7-3x.

Also removed the `wave` reward multiplier entirely: work is already exponential in tier and linear in count, so multiplying it again by an unbounded order counter was credit inflation stacked on a real curve. Collection TARGETS now grow only slightly (8 + level/2, capped at 20) because a collection is paid for in energy and recharge waiting - doubling it makes it twice as long, not twice as interesting.

**Crates open on the board, one tap at a time (Codex).** Verified complete: `showCrateModal` and `applyCrate` are gone, `tapCrate` dispenses one payload entry per tap, item entries fly out via `playSpawnFrom` into the nearest empty ring and trigger the locked-match hint, currency entries use the `floatingScore` popup, and the crate is consumed when empty.

**Crates are items, not a separate list (Codex).** Verified complete: the `rewards.crates` array and CRATES button are gone; crates live on the board and in the inventory, and are draggable, sellable (`sellSelectedCrate`) and storable, carrying their remaining contents so a part-emptied crate never refills.

- **Daily login crates are wired (crate feed 3 of 3).** The player profile now shows today's exact crate tier and resulting streak before claim, or a claimed/return-tomorrow state afterward. Daily availability contributes to the level-badge ready count and is announced non-modally on load. - **A matching item landing near a locked tile now announces itself.** Both the new item AND the locked tile it could clear pulse and show the merge-ready ring. Previously the opportunity was very easy to miss: locked tiles are deliberately dimmed and sit still, so a matching item arriving elsewhere on a busy board signalled nothing. Hinting only the new item would have left the player hunting for what it matched - the point is to connect the two, so both fire. Reuses the merge-ready ring rather than inventing a cue: that ring already means "these two can merge" everywhere else, so nothing new has to be learned; the scale pulses are what catch the eye. Hooked into `placeTile` so it covers produced drops, merge results, crate items and shop buys alike, and never fires on load (which passes `animateIn: false`). Delayed 200ms past the settle-in tween so it reads as a separate event rather than smearing into the arrival.

- **Crate system + the output meter (feed 1 of 3).** New `src/game/rewards/Rewards.ts` holds ONE payload format and ONE loot table; the output meter, level milestones and the daily claim are three FEEDS into it rather than three parallel reward systems that would drift apart. All three feeds are now wired through one crate payout flow.
- **The meter is the honest version of the fill-a-meter-and-pop mechanic** from slot games (the "3 pigs" pattern). In those, the fill is cosmetic and the payout is decided elsewhere, so the player's sense of getting closer is a fiction. Here the fill is the ONLY thing that picks the crate, it is fully deterministic, and the thresholds are drawn as visible notches. Variance lives in the crate's CONTENTS, never in whether progress counted. That split - certainty about progress, variance in reward - keeps the anticipation without the deception, and it is the only version that belongs in a game whose pitch is a precise, learnable machine.
- **Cashing out early is a real decision, not a formality.** Collecting costs energy and crates contain energy, so when the bar is low, taking bronze now to keep playing genuinely beats holding for a gold you cannot afford to reach. Without that tension every player would simply always wait for gold.
- Rendered as a machined hairline across the header with a notch per threshold, per the art brief's "industrial techno = a precise indicator, never the subject" - it also cost almost no vertical space, which the header did not have. Label states the earned crate AND the distance to the next, so the cash-out choice is fully informed.
- Claiming refuses (without consuming the meter) when the board cannot hold the crate's items, matching the shop's existing full-board behaviour rather than silently dropping loot.
- Gems in crates are deliberately scarce and tiny - 8% chance of 1 at bronze, and only the vault can ever pay 5. They price the gem shop row and the 40-gem energy refill; handing them out freely would quietly make both meaningless.
- 25 tests over the reward logic, including that meter progress is deterministic (if it ever needed an RNG it would have become the thing it was written to avoid), that a missed day restarts the daily streak at 1 rather than zeroing it, and that item tiers track player level so a deep player never opens a crate full of gravel.

- **The order bar now says it scrolls.** Two cues, because the edge fades alone read as the board panel overlapping the cards rather than as content continuing past the edge. (1) A track + thumb under the cards, reusing the SAME idiom the shop's scrolling list already has (4-5px, `Theme.bg` track, light thumb) turned on its side, so the game keeps one scroll language instead of growing a second - and the thumb also answers "how much more is there", which an arrow could not. (2) A one-time nudge on first sight of an overflowing bar: it slides a little and settles back, which is the cue that says DRAGGABLE rather than merely wider than the screen. Fires once per session, never while the player is touching the bar, and never if they have already scrolled it. The hint graphics is created with the order bar (before `drawBoardBackground` runs), so it is explicitly raised above the board glass or it renders underneath.

- **Order cards name the actual item and size themselves to their content.** Requirement rows read `CRYSTAL LATTICE 1/1` rather than `GLASS 07` - the compact family code saved width but made the player translate a code into the thing on their board. Cards are no longer a fixed third of the board width: each measures its own title and rows, then clamps to 104-212px, laid out with a running cursor. Every card now owns a container with its children at LOCAL coordinates, which is what makes both the variable width and the slide below possible.
- **Completable orders slide to the front instead of jumping.** Reordering uses the same 140ms `Quad.Out` as `TileView.snapTo`, so an order moving reads like two board pieces trading places - the one move-animation the player already knows.
- **Two position bugs fixed while doing it, both from card position and queue slot having diverged:**
  - `consumeOrderItems` flew delivered items at the card zone's own x/y, which became LOCAL when cards moved into per-card containers; it now resolves world space via `orderCardWorldCenter`.
  - `completeOrder` placed its reward popup from the QUEUE slot using the old fixed `(boardWidth - gaps) / 3` card width, so with ready-first sorting the `+CR/+XP` popped over an unrelated card (or off the end of the bar). It now captures the completed card's world centre BEFORE `refreshOrderBar` re-sorts and re-lays out the bar.
- Reward popup spacing widened from +/-22 to +/-34: rewards scale with the delivered tier now, and a four-digit credit figure collided with the XP figure at spacing chosen for flat two-digit rewards.

- **Completable orders sort to the left of the queue.** A stable partition, not a re-sort: ready and unready orders each keep their queue order, so a card only moves when its OWN readiness changes - anything less stable would shuffle cards while the player is reaching for one. Card position and queue slot now diverge, so every tap resolves through `orderDisplayOrder`; without that mapping a tap on the leftmost card would submit whatever happened to be queue slot 0. The delivery fly-animation takes the display position (it targets the card), while `completeOrder` takes the order index (position-independent). If an order becomes ready while the bar is scrolled away from the left it eases back to 0 - but only when the ready COUNT rises, so idle browsing is never yanked around, and a drag beginning mid-tween stops it.

- **Orders rebuilt: level-gated tiers, multi-item requirements, and a scrolling queue.**
  - **Tier now tracks PLAYER LEVEL, not order count.** The old generator advanced a tier only every 20 completed orders via a `wave` counter, so a player 23 generated orders deep was still being asked for tier 4-6 and had never seen the high tiers at all. `maxOrderTier(level)` now reaches the tier-9 capstones at level 12, and `minOrderTier` trails it by three so a deep player stops being asked for gravel.
  - **Glass bug fixed.** `generateOrder` picked `cycle === 1 || cycle === 4 ? 'mineral' : 'wood'` and never once named glass, so an entire family was invisible to the order economy past the 13 authored starters. Families now come from the canonical `CHAINS` registry - a private copy is exactly how this happened.
  - **Orders can demand several different items.** `OrderDef.requirements` is now a list of `{ typeId, tier, count }`. Lines are drawn WITHOUT replacement so two can never name the same family - `countAtTier` would otherwise satisfy both from one pile and complete the order at half cost. Gated by level: 1 line under level 4, 2 at 4-8, 3 at 9+.
  - **Rewards now pay for the WORK.** Two tier-N items merge into one tier-N+1, so an item's cost in raw drops is `2^(tier-1)`; the old flat `85 + wave * 22` paid the same for a Gravel as for a Sapphire, which is why a high-tier order was never worth wanting. The 3x multiplier is reverse-engineered from the hand-authored starters, which already sit almost exactly on `3 x 2^(tier-1)`.
  - **More than three simultaneous orders, scrolled horizontally.** `orderSlotsForLevel` opens a 4th at level 4 and one more every 4 levels to a cap of 6. The bar is a masked container flicked sideways, with edge fades as the only affordance (a scrollbar would cost a card's worth of height). Card taps use the shop's tap-vs-drag rule so flicking to a later order can't fire whichever card the flick started on.
  - **Orders store the level they were ISSUED at** (`activeOrderLevels`, parallel to `activeOrderIndices`). Deriving it live would silently rewrite an order a player was part-way through the moment they levelled up.
  - Card layout reworked for the above: one requirement per row using the compact `WOOD 04` form the action tray already uses (a proper item name is ~3x wider for no extra information), tinted per family and green once satisfied. Rewards keep two fixed bottom rows - putting three reward tokens on one line forced them to scale to ~0.7 at the smallest cell size, the same small-glyph mush the tier badges hit.
- **Energy panel restyled dark and linked to the gem store.** It was the light `Theme.panel`, the one surface in the palette the resource colours do NOT work on - energy cyan and gem violet are both tuned for dark grounds, so the panel's own subject matter was the least legible thing on it. Now `bgElevated` with the standard lit top edge, plus a GET GEMS action that dismisses and opens the shop's gem row directly (a refill is priced in gems, so the panel was a dead end for anyone without them - exactly the player who is there).

- **Source reservoir capacity is now flat across tiers - a FAMILY trait, not a tier one.** Wood ran 30 -> 14 across its five source tiers and glass ran 18, 21, 24, 18, 20 - climbing, dropping, then climbing again. Neither was a designed curve; both were the retired batch-based config's `dropsPerCharge x maxCharges` multiplied out, and the glass wobble was pure artifact. Upgrading therefore handed the player a smaller number than they had - the merge-satisfaction principle violated on the persistent layer they are meant to want to keep. Fixed to a flat wood 30 / mineral 10 / glass 18, with `capacityForTier` now ignoring tier the same way `cooldownForTier` already does.
- Growing capacity with tier was tried first and **reverted, correctly**: a bigger reservoir means more banked drops, which means the player reaches the recharge timer LESS often - and that short wait is the most satisfying beat in the game. It would have paid twice for an upgrade that is already generous (a tier-2 source drops tier-2 items, worth a whole merge step more on every tap, forever) with the second payment taken out of the recharge cadence. Capacity and recharge rate are family traits; tier is the capability. Keeping them separate is the design. Three tests pin it: flat across tiers, the authored wood-30 opening, and the three families staying distinct from each other.
